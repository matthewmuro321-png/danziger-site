Attorneys

A practice built over decades.

The attorneys of Danziger, Danziger & Muro bring decades of experience advising businesses, investors, entrepreneurs, and institutions on corporate transactions and day-to-day legal matters. The firm's longstanding team combines broad transactional experience with international perspective and the continuity of lawyers who have worked together for many years.

Thomas C. Danziger is a founding partner of Danziger, Danziger & Muro and a nationally recognized art lawyer. For more than three decades, he has advised participants throughout the art market on transactions and legal issues involving the purchase and sale of art, auctions, financing, intellectual property, and other commercial matters. His clients have included collectors, galleries, dealers, museums, artists, financial institutions, and other art-market businesses and institutions.

Thomas is ranked Band 1 nationally for Art and Cultural Property Law by Chambers High Net Worth 2026, which notes his reputation in the art market and his work on significant transactional matters. He is also frequently sought out as a commentator on developments affecting the art market and has written and spoken on subjects including auction regulation, sanctions, taxation, authenticity, cultural property, and the international movement of art.

Before founding the firm, Thomas practiced in the Real Estate Department at Shearman & Sterling in New York. He graduated from Wesleyan University with honors in general scholarship and from NYU School of Law, where he was an editor of the Journal of International Law and Politics. He is fluent in French.

CTD

Charles T. Danziger

Partner

charles@danziger.com

Yale University, magna cum laude
NYU School of Law

Charles T. Danziger is a founding partner of Danziger, Danziger & Muro whose practice is distinguished by decades of experience in art law and international business, particularly matters involving Japan and the broader Asian market. He has worked with his brother Thomas in art law for more than 30 years and has advised clients at the intersection of art, commerce, intellectual property, and cross-border transactions.

Charles brings an unusually international background to his practice. Before founding the firm, he practiced in the Banking, Corporate, and Asia Corporate groups at Milbank and at Nagashima & Ohno in Tokyo. He later served as Assistant General Counsel of The Museum of Modern Art, giving him direct institutional experience in the art world as well as private-practice experience.

A longtime student of Japanese culture, Charles is the author and illustrator of Japan for Starters: 52 Things You Need to Know About Japan and the author of Harvey and Etsuko's Manga Guide to Japan. He has lectured at the University of Chicago Law School and Keio University and serves on the board of Partners in Performance, founded by violinist Midori. He is fluent in Japanese, German, and French and conversational in Korean.

Charles graduated magna cum laude from Yale University with a B.A. in art history and received his J.D. from NYU School of Law, where he was an editor of the Journal of International Law and Politics. He has been recognized by The Best Lawyers in America for Art Law since 2025.

Bradley J. Muro advises companies, investors, and business owners on mergers and acquisitions, corporate finance, joint ventures, private equity and venture capital transactions, and a broad range of ongoing corporate matters. His practice also encompasses real estate, intellectual property, licensing, franchising, distribution, and domestic and cross-border transactions. He has particular experience working with privately held companies and businesses in the luxury goods sector.

Brad brings both private-practice and in-house experience to his work. Before joining Danziger, Danziger & Muro, he served as Vice President and General Counsel of LaunchCenter 39, an internet incubator and venture capital company. Earlier in his career, he practiced mergers and acquisitions, corporate finance, and venture capital at Gibson Dunn and executive compensation and employee benefits at Skadden.

Brad received his B.A., cum laude, from Duke University and his J.D. from NYU School of Law. He has been admitted to practice in New York since 1995.

Karen Hirshfield has extensive experience advising private companies and investors on mergers and acquisitions, venture capital and private equity transactions, and day-to-day corporate and commercial matters. She has represented both buyers and sellers in M&A transactions and advises businesses on entity formation, commercial agreements, corporate governance, and other matters arising throughout the life of a company.

Before joining Danziger, Danziger & Muro, Karen was counsel in the Corporate Finance and Securities Group at Lowenstein Sandler. Earlier, she practiced in the New Media Group at Frankfurt Kurnit Klein & Selz, focusing on private equity transactions, and in the Corporate Group at Gibson Dunn.

Karen graduated from the University of Michigan and received her J.D., cum laude, from NYU School of Law, where she served as a staff editor of the Annual Survey of American Law.

Kate Yuhkyoung Rhie is a New York attorney whose practice focuses on art law, intellectual property, and cross-border matters involving Korea and the international art market. She has advised on art transactions and related intellectual property issues and works with Korean clients navigating legal and commercial matters involving the United States and Korea.

Kate has particular knowledge of the Korean art market and has written publicly about the legal and regulatory issues affecting galleries, collectors, and other market participants doing business in Korea. Her recent writing has addressed Korean art fairs, customs and tax treatment, cultural-property restrictions, and cross-border art transactions. She has also written with Thomas and Charles Danziger on the Korean art market.

Kate studied law at Seoul National University and received M.A. and Ph.D. degrees from Korea University, with graduate work in jurisprudence and cultural heritage law. She also graduated from Northwestern University School of Law. She has held academic positions in Korea in addition to her legal practice.
