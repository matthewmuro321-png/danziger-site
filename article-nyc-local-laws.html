New York City Local Laws and Leasing Issues: Local Law 97, Local Law 11, and Practical Lease Drafting

Real Estate · 2024

Introduction

New York City leasing is increasingly shaped by building compliance laws. A modern lease must do more than allocate rent, possession, maintenance, assignment rights, and default remedies. Local law compliance now affects operating expenses, capital projects, utility data, access rights, facade work, sidewalk sheds, signage, business interruption, insurance, and the allocation of fines and penalties.

Two recurring examples are Local Law 97 and Local Law 11. Local Law 97 addresses building greenhouse gas emissions. Local Law 11, now administered through the Facade Inspection and Safety Program, addresses periodic exterior wall inspections and related facade safety work. Although the primary statutory obligation usually falls on the building owner, both laws can materially affect the economic and operational bargain between landlord and tenant.

Local Law 97: Why It Matters in Leasing

Local Law 97 generally requires most New York City buildings over 25,000 square feet to meet greenhouse gas emissions limits beginning in 2024, with more stringent limits beginning in 2030. New York City Department of Buildings guidance also identifies an excess emissions penalty formula for certain covered buildings based on actual emissions minus the applicable emissions limit, multiplied by $268 per year.

For landlords, this means the lease should address cooperation, access, submetering, data collection, tenant equipment, after hours use, supplemental HVAC, and the pass through treatment of compliance costs. For tenants, the lease should prevent the landlord from converting general building compliance into an unlimited tenant cost, particularly where penalties or capital work result from the landlord’s failure to plan, maintain, file, or comply.

The practical issue is not simply whether the building is covered. The issue is whether the tenant’s use affects the building’s emissions profile and whether the lease clearly allocates the costs and risks of compliance.

Local Law 97: Landlord Favorable Lease Language

Local Law 97 Compliance. Tenant shall reasonably cooperate with Landlord in connection with Landlord’s compliance with all present and future laws, rules, regulations, codes, orders, benchmarking requirements, energy reporting requirements, building performance standards, and greenhouse gas emissions requirements applicable to the building, including New York City Local Law 97, as amended from time to time.

Tenant Data and Cooperation. Tenant shall, within ten days after request, provide Landlord with utility data, submetering information, equipment schedules, occupancy information, operating schedules, supplemental system information, and other information reasonably required for compliance, reporting, benchmarking, filing, penalty mitigation, or building performance analysis. Tenant authorizes Landlord to request and receive utility information relating to the premises directly from applicable utility providers to the extent permitted by law.

Tenant Operations. Tenant shall not install or operate equipment, fixtures, supplemental HVAC, refrigeration, cooking equipment, data processing equipment, or other systems, and shall not conduct operations or maintain operating hours, in a manner that materially increases building energy consumption, peak demand, or greenhouse gas emissions beyond the level reasonably contemplated by the permitted use under this lease, without Landlord’s prior written consent. Landlord may condition consent upon Tenant’s agreement to pay the incremental costs, charges, penalties, compliance costs, or emissions impacts reasonably attributable to such use.

Access and Compliance Work. Landlord may enter the premises upon reasonable prior notice, except in an emergency, to inspect, meter, submeter, test, maintain, repair, replace, upgrade, alter, or install equipment, controls, meters, submeters, lighting, HVAC components, windows, insulation, electrical systems, mechanical systems, and other improvements reasonably related to legal compliance, energy efficiency, emissions reduction, or building performance. Landlord shall use commercially reasonable efforts to minimize unreasonable interference with Tenant’s business operations.

Cost Allocation. Costs incurred by Landlord in connection with benchmarking, emissions reporting, compliance filings, engineering studies, commissioning, retro commissioning, energy audits, energy efficiency measures, metering, submetering, and legally required building performance work shall be included in operating expenses to the extent not expressly excluded elsewhere in this lease. Capital improvements required by law or reasonably intended to reduce energy consumption, reduce greenhouse gas emissions, reduce operating expenses, or maintain legal compliance may be included in operating expenses if amortized over the useful life of the improvement, together with interest at the rate provided in this lease.

Tenant Caused Penalties. If Tenant’s acts, omissions, equipment, operating hours, utility consumption, refusal to provide information, delay in providing access, or breach of this lease causes or materially contributes to a violation, exceedance, fine, penalty, assessment, charge, or loss of mitigation opportunity, Tenant shall reimburse Landlord for the portion reasonably attributable to Tenant.

Local Law 97: Tenant Favorable Lease Language

Local Law 97 Compliance. Landlord shall be responsible, at Landlord’s sole cost except to the extent expressly included in operating expenses under this lease, for compliance with all laws applicable to the building, including New York City Local Law 97, as amended from time to time. Tenant shall reasonably cooperate with Landlord by providing information in Tenant’s possession and control that is reasonably necessary for required filings, provided that Landlord’s requests are reasonable, not unduly burdensome, and made on reasonable prior notice.

Data Limits and Confidentiality. Tenant shall not be required to provide proprietary, competitively sensitive, or confidential operational information except to the extent reasonably necessary for legal compliance. Landlord shall keep Tenant’s nonpublic information confidential and shall use such information only for building compliance, reporting, benchmarking, and related purposes.

No Uncapped Pass Through. Operating expenses shall not include fines, penalties, interest, late charges, excess emissions penalties, or similar charges arising under Local Law 97 or any similar law, except to the extent directly caused by Tenant’s breach of this lease after notice and a reasonable opportunity to cure. Operating expenses shall not include costs incurred to correct Landlord’s failure to maintain, repair, operate, file, report, or comply with law in a timely manner.

Capital Improvements. No capital improvement, capital replacement, electrification project, building system upgrade, or emissions reduction project shall be included in operating expenses unless the lease expressly permits such inclusion. Any permitted capital item shall be amortized over its useful life in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, and Tenant shall only pay the portion allocable to the term of this lease and Tenant’s proportionate share of the building.

Access Protection. Landlord’s access to the premises for Local Law 97 related work shall be subject to reasonable prior notice, reasonable scheduling, Tenant’s security requirements, and commercially reasonable efforts to avoid interference with Tenant’s business. Landlord shall promptly repair any damage caused by such entry or work. If access or compliance work materially prevents Tenant from using a material portion of the premises for more than three consecutive business days, Fixed Rent and additional rent shall abate in proportion to the affected area until reasonable use is restored.

Tenant Use Safe Harbor. Tenant’s use of the premises in accordance with the permitted use, ordinary business hours, and equipment approved by Landlord as of the commencement date shall not be deemed to cause a Local Law 97 violation or penalty. Tenant shall have no liability for building wide emissions exceedances except to the extent caused by Tenant’s particular use or equipment in excess of the approved baseline and only after Landlord provides reasonable supporting documentation.

Local Law 11 and FISP: Why It Matters in Leasing

Local Law 11 is commonly used to refer to New York City’s facade inspection regime, now administered through the Facade Inspection and Safety Program. Owners of buildings higher than six stories must have exterior walls and appurtenances inspected every five years and must file a technical facade report with the Department of Buildings. The required inspection work and any resulting repairs can affect tenants through scaffolding, sidewalk sheds, window protection, access limitations, terrace closures, noise, dust, vibration, signage obstruction, and reduced storefront visibility.

Facade issues are especially important for restaurants, retail tenants, medical offices, showrooms, schools, hospitality users, and tenants with ground floor signage, outdoor seating, terraces, balconies, or customer facing entrances. A lease should state whether facade work is a normal building condition that does not create a rent abatement, or whether prolonged interference triggers tenant remedies.

Local Law 11: Landlord Favorable Lease Language

Facade Compliance. Tenant acknowledges that the building may be subject to periodic facade inspection, maintenance, repair, replacement, and safety requirements, including the New York City Facade Inspection and Safety Program, commonly known as Local Law 11, as amended from time to time. Landlord may perform all inspections, testing, probes, repairs, replacements, protective measures, filings, and related work required or recommended in connection with such requirements.

Scaffolding and Sidewalk Protection. Landlord may install, maintain, alter, and remove sidewalk sheds, scaffolding, netting, bridging, barriers, protection, sidewalk closures, window protection, and other safety measures in, on, over, or adjacent to the building, the premises, entrances, storefronts, windows, terraces, roof areas, sidewalks, and other portions of the building. The presence of such items shall not constitute an eviction, constructive eviction, breach of quiet enjoyment, actual or constructive taking, or grounds for rent abatement, except to the extent expressly provided in this lease.

Access. Tenant shall provide Landlord and Landlord’s contractors, consultants, architects, engineers, inspectors, and representatives with reasonable access to the premises and any exterior or appurtenant areas for facade inspections, probes, repairs, testing, maintenance, safety work, and compliance work. Tenant shall not obstruct, delay, or interfere with such work.

No Liability for Business Interruption. Landlord shall use commercially reasonable efforts to minimize unreasonable interference with Tenant’s access to the premises, but Landlord shall not be liable for loss of business, loss of profits, diminished visibility, inconvenience, noise, dust, vibration, obstruction, or interference arising from facade work, sidewalk sheds, scaffolding, or legal compliance work, except to the extent caused by Landlord’s gross negligence or willful misconduct.

Tenant Delay. Tenant shall reimburse Landlord for any loss, cost, fine, penalty, contractor charge, delay claim, or additional expense arising from Tenant’s failure to provide access or Tenant’s interference with facade related work.

Local Law 11: Tenant Favorable Lease Language

Facade Work Notice. Landlord shall provide Tenant with not less than thirty days’ prior written notice of any planned facade work, sidewalk shed installation, scaffolding, exterior wall inspection, terrace closure, window protection, or other exterior work reasonably expected to affect Tenant’s access, visibility, signage, customer traffic, use, or operations, except in an emergency or where shorter notice is required by law or governmental authority.

Minimization of Interference. Landlord shall perform facade work in a commercially reasonable manner, shall coordinate the work with Tenant in good faith, and shall use commercially reasonable efforts to minimize interference with Tenant’s access, signage, visibility, deliveries, customer traffic, security, and business operations. Landlord shall maintain safe pedestrian access to the premises at all times except to the extent prohibited by law or emergency conditions.

Temporary Signage. If scaffolding, sidewalk sheds, netting, bridging, or other protective measures materially obstruct Tenant’s signage, storefront, entrance, display windows, or street visibility, Landlord shall, at Landlord’s cost, use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain approval for and provide reasonable temporary signage on the sidewalk shed or other protective structure identifying Tenant’s business.

Rent Abatement. If facade work, scaffolding, sidewalk sheds, or related protective measures materially impair access to the premises or materially prevent Tenant from conducting business in a material portion of the premises for more than five consecutive business days, Fixed Rent and additional rent shall abate equitably in proportion to the degree and duration of the impairment until the impairment is cured. If the impairment continues for more than one hundred twenty days, Tenant may terminate this lease upon thirty days’ written notice unless the impairment is cured before the termination date.

No Waiver of Claims. Nothing in this lease shall limit Tenant’s rights or remedies for Landlord’s negligence, willful misconduct, failure to comply with law, failure to maintain required insurance, or unreasonable failure to prosecute facade work diligently after commencement.

Operating Expense Drafting

Operating expense drafting is the area where many local law disputes arise. General language requiring tenants to pay legal compliance costs may not answer the most important questions. Does the clause include capital improvements? Does it include engineering studies? Does it include penalties? Does it include costs caused by pre existing violations? Does it include work that benefits the building beyond the tenant’s term?

Landlord Favorable Operating Expense Language

Subject to the express exclusions in this lease, operating expenses shall include all reasonable costs and expenses incurred by Landlord in connection with compliance with laws affecting the ownership, operation, maintenance, repair, safety, energy performance, emissions performance, facade condition, and legal compliance of the building, including inspections, reports, filings, professional fees, engineering fees, legal compliance consulting, energy benchmarking, emissions reporting, facade inspections, facade repairs, safety measures, metering, submetering, commissioning, retro commissioning, energy audits, and governmental compliance programs.

Operating expenses shall include the amortized cost of capital improvements required by law or reasonably intended to reduce operating expenses, reduce energy consumption, reduce greenhouse gas emissions, satisfy building performance standards, mitigate penalties, or maintain compliance with applicable law, amortized over the useful life reasonably determined by Landlord, together with interest at the rate set forth in this lease.

Tenant Favorable Operating Expense Language

Operating expenses shall exclude fines, penalties, late charges, interest, excess emissions penalties, violation removal costs, and similar charges arising from Landlord’s failure to comply with law, timely file required reports, timely perform required work, or maintain the building in compliance with applicable law.

Operating expenses shall exclude capital expenditures except to the extent expressly permitted in this lease. Any permitted capital expenditure shall be amortized over its useful life in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, shall be limited to Tenant’s proportionate share, and shall be included only for the portion of the amortization period falling within the term of this lease. In no event shall Tenant be required to pay for capital work that corrects a condition existing before the commencement date, except to the extent expressly disclosed in writing before lease execution.

Due Diligence Before Lease Signing

Both parties should address local law compliance before the lease is signed. A landlord should confirm that the tenant’s proposed use is consistent with the building’s compliance strategy. A tenant should confirm whether known compliance work will affect cost, access, visibility, buildout, use, or occupancy.

Tenant Questions

Is the building subject to Local Law 97?

Is the building currently in compliance with applicable emissions limits and reporting requirements?

Has the owner received notices, violations, or penalty assessments relating to Local Law 97?

What energy efficiency, electrification, metering, submetering, or building system work is planned during the lease term?

Will Local Law 97 costs be included in operating expenses?

Is the building subject to FISP or Local Law 11 facade filing requirements?

Are facade inspections, unsafe conditions, sidewalk sheds, scaffolding, or facade repairs expected during the lease term?

Will work affect entrances, windows, signage, outdoor dining, terraces, deliveries, or customer access?

Landlord Questions

Does the tenant’s permitted use involve high energy consumption, extended hours, commercial cooking, refrigeration, laboratory equipment, medical equipment, data rooms, or supplemental HVAC?

Will the tenant provide utility data and consent to submetering?

Does the lease reserve sufficient access rights for inspections, upgrades, facade work, and compliance work?

Does the alteration clause require efficient equipment and prohibit installations that impair building compliance?

Does the operating expense clause clearly address compliance costs, capital costs, penalties, exclusions, and tenant caused costs?

Does the signage and access language account for future scaffolding or sidewalk sheds?

Practical Takeaways

Local Law 97 and Local Law 11 are not only regulatory issues. They are leasing issues. They affect the economics and operation of the tenancy, particularly in larger buildings, high energy uses, ground floor retail spaces, restaurant spaces, medical offices, and leases with long terms.

The strongest lease forms now address local law compliance directly. A landlord favorable form should preserve access, cooperation, data rights, pass through rights, and reimbursement for tenant caused compliance impacts. A tenant favorable form should limit pass throughs, exclude penalties, require notice, protect access and signage, and provide abatement for prolonged material interference.

The best drafting does not rely on a generic comply with law clause. It identifies the likely compliance issues, assigns responsibility for information and access, allocates costs and penalties, and creates a practical process for work that may interfere with occupancy.
