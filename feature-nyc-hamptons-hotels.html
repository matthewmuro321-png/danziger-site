Hospitality · Hotels

Hotel Happenings: NYC & the Hamptons

New openings, redesigns, restaurants, rooftops, and summer programming across the city and the East End.

Hospitality · Market Notes

New York hospitality is having an active 2026 season. In the city, the hotel story is about new lifestyle towers, destination restaurants, restored landmarks, rooftops, and hotels becoming social spaces again. Out East, the Hamptons are leaning into refreshed inns, beach clubs, resort-style wellness, waterfront dining, and seasonal programming.

New York City

Kimpton Era Midtown New York

32 West 48th Street, Midtown · Opened March 2026

A 500-plus-room lifestyle hotel near Rockefeller Center, with multiple food-and-beverage outlets including Bar Rocco (chef Rocco DiSpirito) and a rooftop izakaya concept.

Best for: Rockefeller Center stays, business travel, and theater weekends.

Faena New York

One High Line, West Chelsea · Opened September 2025

The Faena brand in West Chelsea, known for art-forward interiors, theatrical hospitality, and food, beverage, and wellness programming that draws locals as well as guests.

Best for: West Chelsea weekends, gallery trips, and design-minded luxury stays.

Hotel Chelsea

222 West 23rd Street, Chelsea

The restored 1880s landmark, now a stay and a food-and-beverage destination with Caf&eacute; Chelsea, the revived El Quijote, and The Lobby Bar.

Best for: Chelsea stays, classic New York atmosphere, and a hotel with real cultural history.

The Fifth Avenue Hotel

1 West 28th Street, NoMad

A historic building joined to a new tower, known for maximalist design and destination dining at chef Andrew Carmellini&rsquo;s Caf&eacute; Carmellini.

Best for: Design-focused stays, special-occasion dining, and NoMad weekends.

Nine Orchard

9 Orchard Street, Lower East Side

A boutique hotel in the former Jarmulowsky Bank building, with the Swan Room cocktail bar and Corner Bar restaurant and easy access to the Lower East Side and downtown.

Best for: Boutique stays, downtown dining, and historic architecture.

The Ned NoMad

1170 Broadway, NoMad

A club-like hotel with members-club energy and multiple food-and-beverage spaces, part of The Ned brand that began in London.

Best for: Business-social crossover, NoMad stays, and a hotel that is part of the night out.

The Carlyle

35 East 76th Street, Upper East Side

One of New York&rsquo;s defining grand hotels and an active cultural venue, home to Bemelmans Bar, Caf&eacute; Carlyle, and the invite-only Tony Awards afterparty.

Best for: Uptown luxury, Bemelmans Bar, and special-occasion stays.

Times Square Rooftops

Midtown

Newer rooftop concepts, such as Voco Roofbar Times Square, aiming to give the neighborhood something more polished than its old tourist-bar reputation.

Best for: Pre-theater drinks, skyline views, and hotel-bar energy.

The Hamptons

The Hedges Inn

74 James Lane, East Hampton · Refreshed for summer 2026

An established East Hampton inn reimagined with interiors by David Netto, with Swifty&rsquo;s returning and added hospitality touches like in-room dining and beach service.

Best for: East Hampton stays, classic village atmosphere, and design-conscious guests.

Montauk Yacht Club

32 Star Island Road, Montauk

A historic 1929 resort on Star Island with marina and waterfront, adding Alba Spiaggia (Prince Street Hospitality), padel and pickleball, a floating wellness cabana, and surf programming for 2026.

Best for: Resort stays, marina culture, families, and wellness weekends.

Barlume Beach

Montauk · Opened summer 2026

A beach-club and hotel concept with Mediterranean food, music, and a coastal social atmosphere, reflecting Montauk&rsquo;s shift toward design-forward hospitality.

Best for: Beach-club energy, Mediterranean food, and social Montauk weekends.

Faraway Sag Harbor

Sag Harbor · Opening summer 2026

The Faraway brand in a walkable Hamptons village, with maritime-inspired design and an Italian seaside restaurant, Zagara, on-site.

Best for: Sag Harbor weekends, walkable stays, and Italian coastal dining.

The Capri Hotel

281 County Road 39A, Southampton

A social, nightlife-forward Southampton hotel drawing renewed attention through Maison Close, the French restaurant and nightlife concept.

Best for: Southampton weekends, nightlife, pool days, and livelier stays.

Baker House 1650

181 Main Street, East Hampton

A boutique inn with historic architecture, gardens, and spa elements, centered on privacy and service rather than beach-club noise.

Best for: Couples, quiet luxury, and historic East Hampton village access.

Marram Montauk

21 Oceanview Terrace, Montauk

The softer, design-conscious side of Montauk: ocean access, quiet rooms, and a nature-forward style for travelers who want a calmer alternative.

Best for: Ocean access, understated stays, and wellness-minded travelers.

Gurney&rsquo;s Montauk Resort &amp; Seawater Spa

290 Old Montauk Highway, Montauk

The recognizable full-service beach resort with scale, beachfront, spa, dining, and events infrastructure that smaller inns cannot match.

Best for: Full-service beach resort stays, spa weekends, and weddings.

What the Two Scenes Share

In both markets, hotels are acting less like places to sleep and more like social platforms. In the city that means restaurants, cocktail bars, rooftops, private rooms, and restored historic spaces; in the Hamptons it means beach service, floating spas, racket sports, chef-driven restaurants, and beach clubs. The best choice now depends less on room type than on how you want to use the property.
