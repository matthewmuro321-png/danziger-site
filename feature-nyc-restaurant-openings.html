New NYC Restaurant Openings to Know

A look at the city’s newest restaurant openings: the formats, the operators, and what they signal about where New York dining is heading.

Hospitality · Market Notes

New York City’s newest restaurant openings span a floating bar on a retired FDNY fireboat, a Mexico City taco import, a Northern Vietnamese kitchen from a Michelin-recognized Dallas operator, a permanent East Harlem home for a Puerto Rican pop-up, a Roman-style pizzeria in Chelsea, and one of Midtown’s splashiest steakhouse openings in years.

Opening dates, hours, menus, and reservation policies change quickly, so check each restaurant directly before going.

Fireboat

Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6, Brooklyn Heights · Opened June 2026

A retired 1960s FDNY fireboat converted into a floating bar by the Pincus brothers and Crew (Grand Banks, Yacht Club), serving tropical cocktails and Caribbean-leaning food with harbor views. Old hoses are repurposed as tap fixtures; seating is walk-in, with the upper deck bookable for events.

Best for: Summer drinks, harbor views, casual dates, and group drinks.

Tacos Domingo

131 First Avenue, East Village · Opened June 2026

A Mexico City import focused on carne asada, with a tightly focused menu of grilled-beef tacos in a high-foot-traffic stretch near St. Mark&rsquo;s Place.

Best for: Carne asada tacos, casual East Village dinners, and late-night eating.

Ngon Vietnamese Kitchen

85 Second Avenue, East Village · Opened June 2026

The New York outpost of Carol Nguy&#7877;n&rsquo;s Michelin Bib Gourmand Dallas restaurant, with a Northern Vietnamese focus that goes beyond the standard pho-and-banh-mi template.

Best for: Casual dinner, Vietnamese food fans, and a downtown option with an out-of-town following.

Cocotazo

349 East 109th Street, East Harlem · Opened June 2026

Chef Frances Roman&rsquo;s first permanent restaurant after years at Queens Night Market and La Marqueta, serving slow-roasted pernil, cod fritters, guava barbecue ribs, and house-made condensed-milk ice cream.

Best for: Puerto Rican comfort food, casual meals, and East Harlem dining.

Forno D&rsquo;Oro

196 Eighth Avenue, Chelsea · Opened June 2026

A Roman-style pizzeria from chef Salvatore Olivella, with pies topped in &rsquo;nduja, soppressata, hot honey, burrata, and Meyer-lemon dressing.

Best for: Roman-style pizza, casual Italian, and Chelsea group meals.

Eleanor&rsquo;s Jour Et Nuit

398 Ninth Avenue, Chelsea · Opened June 2026

An all-day-into-evening concept from the Avra group near Hudson Yards and Penn Station, moving from daytime cafe to cocktails, dinner, and DJs.

Best for: Coffee meetings, after-work drinks, and dinner with a scene.

Marcel

The Breuer Building, Upper East Side · Opened 2026

A French restaurant inside the Breuer building, now Sotheby&rsquo;s global headquarters, with Roman and Williams design and a menu of duck confit, c&ocirc;te de boeuf, and Dover sole, all part of a larger cultural and luxury environment.

Best for: Art-adjacent lunches, special occasions, and design-conscious dinners.

Cote 550

550 Madison Avenue, Midtown · Opened April 2026

Simon Kim&rsquo;s Midtown expansion of the Michelin-starred Korean steakhouse, opened with Bar Chimera inside the landmark former AT&amp;T Building, combining Korean barbecue, American steakhouse service, and a large wine program with David Rockwell design.

Best for: Business dinners, celebrations, client meals, and luxury Korean barbecue.

Dean&rsquo;s

213 Sixth Avenue, SoHo · Opened Spring 2026

A walk-in British seafood pub from Jess Shadbolt and Annie Shi of King and Jupiter, serving stargazy pie, fish and chips, oysters, Guinness, and ginger pudding.

Best for: Seafood, oysters and Guinness, walk-in dining, and casual SoHo dinners.

Theodora Beach Club

Governors Island (weekends) · June 2026 residency

A weekends-only Governors Island residency from Resy and Tomer Blechman&rsquo;s Theodora, built around live-fire cooking stations, DJs, and family-style dinners with guest chefs.

Best for: Weekend plans, group dinners, and event-style dining.

Ramen by Ra

East Village · Opened 2026

A Black-owned breakfast-ramen counter from chef Rasheeda Purdie, translating New York breakfast flavors (bacon, egg, and cheese) into ramen form.

Best for: Breakfast or brunch ramen, solo dining, and something less conventional.

What This Group of Openings Shows

Restaurants are increasingly built around specific formats rather than generic dining rooms (a floating bar, a seasonal residency, a walk-in pub, a luxury steakhouse complex), and operators are leaning on strong backstories and physical settings, from a retired FDNY vessel to Sotheby’s Breuer building. The city’s casual openings are as interesting as its expensive ones, and many are tied to experienced operators with real track records.
