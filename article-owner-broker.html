Owner Broker Agreements for NYC Commercial Leasing

A practical owner side guide to the exact issues that should be negotiated before a broker markets commercial space, registers tenants, or claims a commission.

Real Estate · 2023

This article is written for building owners, asset managers, landlords, and counsel who want a broker agreement that supports leasing activity without creating avoidable commission exposure.

Owner takeaway

Do not sign a broker form until the appointment type, covered premises, excluded prospects, tenant registration process, commission base, payment conditions, tail rights, renewals, co broker claims, marketing authority, and termination rights are all clear.

The most expensive disputes usually come from four drafting failures: vague exclusivity, vague tenant registration, vague commission timing, and vague post expiration rights.

A good owner form should pay brokers for real value while preventing double commissions, commissions on owner sourced deals, commissions on rent never paid, and commissions on future transactions that were not part of the bargain.

1. Start With the Business Question

Before discussing commission percentages, the owner should decide what it actually wants the broker to do. A broker agreement for a vacant retail store, a full floor office vacancy, a restaurant space, a medical suite, a warehouse, or a mixed use building should not be identical.

The threshold question is simple: is the owner hiring the broker to be the exclusive market facing leasing agent, or is the owner merely allowing the broker to submit prospects? Everything else flows from that choice.

Owner goal

Match the broker appointment to the owner’s leasing strategy.

Agree to

Use an exclusive agency only if the broker is expected to invest meaningful time in canvassing, marketing, showings, reporting, and deal negotiation.

Compromise

Use a short exclusive agency with strong exclusions, registration rules, and a narrow tail.

Avoid

Avoid signing a broad exclusive right to lease if the owner wants to preserve direct deals, existing tenant negotiations, or other broker relationships.

2. The Three Appointment Types Owners Actually Need to Understand

Exclusive right to lease

This is the most broker protective structure. If a lease is signed during the term, the broker may claim a commission even if the tenant came from the owner, another broker, an existing tenant, or a direct inquiry. Owners should use this only when they truly want to outsource the leasing function and are comfortable paying for any lease during the term, subject to written exclusions.

Exclusive agency

This is often the best compromise for owners. The broker is the exclusive leasing agent, but the owner reserves the right to lease directly to excluded prospects or owner sourced prospects without paying a commission, unless the broker was actually involved or was the procuring cause.

Nonexclusive authorization

This is the safest structure for owners who want brokers to bring prospects but do not want to surrender control. The broker earns a commission only for a tenant that was properly registered, accepted by the owner, and actually produces a signed lease.

Owner favorable appointment clause

Owner appoints Broker solely as a nonexclusive broker authorized to submit prospective tenants for the Premises. Broker shall not be Owner’s exclusive leasing agent and shall not have an exclusive right to lease. Owner may market, negotiate, lease, or license the Premises directly or through other brokers without liability to Broker, except to the extent a commission is expressly earned under this Agreement with respect to an accepted Registered Prospect.

Compromise exclusive agency clause

Owner appoints Broker as exclusive leasing agent for the Premises during the Term. This appointment is an exclusive agency only and not an exclusive right to lease. Owner reserves the right, without obligation to pay Broker a commission, to lease the Premises directly to any Excluded Prospect or to any prospect not introduced, registered, contacted, shown, negotiated with, or procured by Broker.

3. Define the Premises Narrowly

Many owner broker disputes begin because the agreement says the broker is retained for the property, while the owner intended only one suite, one store, or one floor. In NYC buildings, commercial value can sit in office floors, retail stores, cellar space, storage rooms, signage, roof rights, antennas, terraces, parking, kiosks, and temporary license areas. The agreement should say exactly what is covered and what is not.

Owner goal

Limit the appointment to the exact space being marketed.

Agree to

Agree to a schedule showing floor, suite, approximate rentable square feet, permitted uses to be marketed, and asking terms.

Compromise

Allow additional spaces to be added only by signed amendment or written leasing assignment.

Avoid

Avoid appointing a broker for the entire building when the owner intends only a specific vacancy.

Premises covered clause

Broker’s appointment applies only to the premises described on Exhibit A. Broker’s appointment does not apply to any other space, future vacancy, storage area, roof area, signage right, antenna right, parking area, development right, sale transaction, financing transaction, management agreement, license agreement, concession arrangement, or other property interest unless expressly added by written amendment signed by Owner.

4. Excluded Prospects Are Not Boilerplate

The exclusion list is one of the most important owner protections. It prevents commission claims on tenants and prospects that the broker did not create. Without a detailed exclusion list, the owner may pay for a tenant that was already known, already negotiating, already in the building, or already submitted by another broker.

Owners should prepare the exclusion list before signing. It should include existing tenants, affiliates of existing tenants, subtenants, licensees, prior prospects, pending prospects, tenants introduced by other brokers, government or institutional prospects in active procurement, owner affiliates, and any prospect the owner has already contacted.

Owner goal

Preserve all preexisting owner relationships and active negotiations.

Agree to

Agree that excluded prospects are listed on an exhibit and include affiliates, successors, related entities, and existing occupants.

Compromise

Compromise by paying if the owner later asks the broker to materially participate in negotiations with an excluded prospect and agrees in writing.

Avoid

Avoid vague promises that the broker will be paid if it touches an excluded prospect after the agreement begins.

Excluded prospects clause

Broker shall not be entitled to any commission or other compensation with respect to any lease, renewal, extension, expansion, relocation, assignment, sublease, license, amendment, or other occupancy arrangement involving any Excluded Prospect. Excluded Prospects include all persons and entities listed on Exhibit B, all existing tenants, subtenants, licensees, occupants, concessionaires, affiliates of any of the foregoing, prospects previously submitted to Owner by another broker, prospects with whom Owner had substantive discussions before the Effective Date, and prospects that contact Owner directly without Broker’s involvement. Broker shall have no commission right with respect to an Excluded Prospect unless Owner expressly agrees in a separate writing that Broker will be compensated for that specific transaction.

5. Tenant Registration Should Be a Gatekeeping Device

Registration is where many owner forms are too loose. A broker should not be able to send a vague email naming a tenant category and later claim the entire market. The owner should require a complete registration before a showing, before disclosure of confidential information, and before substantive discussions.

A proper registration identifies the legal name, trade name, known affiliates, contact person, tenant representative, proposed use, space involved, and date of introduction. The owner should have the right to reject a registration in writing. Owner forms should not say a registration is automatically accepted unless the owner objects.

Owner goal

Only accepted registrations should create commission eligibility.

Agree to

Agree to review registrations promptly and act in good faith.

Compromise

Compromise by allowing deemed rejection if owner does not respond within five business days, with the broker allowed to resubmit with more detail.

Avoid

Avoid blanket registrations, category registrations, verbal registrations, or automatic acceptance by silence.

Tenant registration clause

Broker shall register each prospective tenant by written notice to Owner before showing the Premises, disclosing nonpublic information, or commencing substantive discussions. The registration notice shall state the prospect’s full legal name, trade name, known affiliates, principal contact, tenant representative, proposed use, and specific Premises involved. No generic, industry wide, category based, or blanket registration shall be effective. A prospect shall become a Registered Prospect only if Owner accepts the registration in writing. Owner may reject a registration if the prospect is an Excluded Prospect, is already known to Owner, was previously submitted by another broker, is already in discussions with Owner, lacks apparent financial capacity, has no bona fide interest, proposes an unsuitable use, or if Owner otherwise rejects the registration in good faith. Unless extended in writing, each accepted registration expires one hundred twenty days after acceptance.

6. Procuring Cause Should Not Be Left to Argument

New York cases often discuss whether a broker was duly licensed, had a contract or implied contract with the party to be charged, and was the procuring cause of the transaction. A recent New York appellate decision restated those elements in a commercial brokerage commission dispute. The better owner approach is to define contractual entitlement more precisely than the default litigation vocabulary.

The owner agreement should state that registration alone is not enough. The broker must have introduced the tenant and materially participated in a continuous chain of activity that resulted in the lease, unless the agreement is intentionally structured as a broader exclusive right.

Owner goal

Require both accepted registration and actual transaction causation.

Agree to

Agree that the broker may be paid when its efforts materially lead to a lease with an accepted registered tenant.

Compromise

Compromise by removing the procuring cause requirement only for a true exclusive right to lease, while preserving exclusions.

Avoid

Avoid paying merely because the broker had a name in its database or sent an old email.

Procuring cause clarification

Registration of a prospect does not by itself entitle Broker to a commission. Broker shall be entitled to a commission only if the prospect is an accepted Registered Prospect, Broker materially participates in a continuous course of activity leading to execution of the lease, and Broker is the procuring cause of the transaction, unless Owner expressly waives such requirement in a separate writing for the specific transaction.

7. Commission Base: Decide What Rent Counts

The commission formula is more than a percentage. The owner must also define the base on which the percentage is applied. In NYC commercial leases, the economic package may include base rent, free rent, abatements, operating expense escalations, real estate tax escalations, electric charges, condenser water charges, after hours HVAC, percentage rent, signage fees, storage charges, parking, tenant improvement reimbursements, termination fees, and other payments.

Owner forms should usually calculate commission only on fixed base rent actually payable during the initial term, excluding free rent, abatements, concessions, additional rent, reimbursements, and other nonbase rent amounts.

Owner goal

Pay commission on the rent that represents the core lease economics, not every dollar that flows through the lease.

Agree to

Agree to a percentage or schedule based on fixed base rent actually payable during the initial term.

Compromise

Compromise by including specified percentage rent only when actually received and only for certain retail deals.

Avoid

Avoid commission on tax escalations, operating expenses, utilities, reimbursements, security deposits, letters of credit, or rent that is waived or never due.

Commission base clause

Any commission payable under this Agreement shall be calculated solely on fixed base rent actually payable by Tenant to Owner during the initial term of the lease. The commission base excludes free rent, rent abatements, concessions, additional rent, real estate tax payments, operating expense payments, common area maintenance charges, insurance charges, utility charges, electric charges, condenser water charges, after hours HVAC charges, percentage rent, tenant improvement reimbursements, signage fees, storage fees, parking fees, late charges, interest, damages, termination payments, security deposits, letters of credit, and all other amounts other than fixed base rent.

8. Commission Timing: Earned Is Different From Payable

The owner should separate when a commission is earned from when it is payable. A broker will usually want the commission earned when the lease is signed. An owner should condition payment on real closing events: signed lease, security delivered, guaranty delivered, insurance delivered, all contingencies satisfied, tenant takes possession, and rent commencement occurs.

This matters because many NYC commercial leases are conditional. A restaurant lease may depend on permits, liquor licensing, venting, or possession delivery. An office lease may require landlord work, tenant improvement allowance documentation, or board approval. A medical lease may require use and licensing review. The owner should not pay the full commission before the tenant actually becomes a performing tenant.

Owner goal

Align commission payment with tenant performance and lease economics.

Agree to

Agree that commission is earned only on lease execution and satisfaction of initial tenant conditions.

Compromise

Compromise by paying part on full execution and the balance at rent commencement or after six months of rent payments.

Avoid

Avoid full commission payment on a letter of intent, draft lease, unsigned lease, conditional lease, or lease where security has not been delivered.

Earned and payable clause

No commission shall be earned or payable unless and until all of the following have occurred: Owner and Tenant have fully executed and delivered a lease for the Premises, Tenant has delivered all required security deposits, letters of credit, guaranties, insurance certificates, initial rent payments, and other items required as conditions to lease effectiveness, all contingencies to lease effectiveness have been satisfied or waived by Owner, and Tenant has taken possession of the Premises. Subject to those conditions, the commission shall be payable fifty percent on the rent commencement date and fifty percent six months after the rent commencement date, provided Tenant is not then in monetary default or material nonmonetary default beyond applicable notice and cure periods.

9. Clawback and Early Termination Protection

Owners should consider what happens if the tenant defaults early, never opens, fails to obtain a license, exercises an early termination right, or the lease terminates before substantial rent is paid. A broker may argue that the lease was signed and the commission was earned. The owner may argue that paying a full commission on a failed lease is commercially unfair. The agreement should answer the question.

Owner goal

Avoid paying commission on lease value that never materializes.

Agree to

Agree to installment payments and a refund for commission attributable to periods after an early termination caused by tenant default or failure of a condition.

Compromise

Compromise by limiting clawback to termination within the first twelve months or before rent commencement.

Avoid

Avoid unconditional commission payment at signing where the tenant has substantial free rent, licensing risk, or an early termination option.

Early termination and refund clause

If the lease terminates before the scheduled expiration date because of Tenant default, failure of a condition, failure to obtain required approvals, exercise of an early termination right, surrender, rejection in bankruptcy, or other termination before the end of the initial term, Broker’s commission shall be limited to the commission attributable to fixed base rent actually payable through the effective termination date. Broker shall refund any commission previously paid with respect to periods after such termination date, except to the extent Owner and Broker expressly agree otherwise in writing for the specific lease.

10. Renewals, Extensions, Expansions, Relocations, and Amendments

Future transaction rights are a major hidden cost. A broker may ask for commissions on renewal terms, extension terms, expansion space, relocation space, holdover arrangements, lease amendments, assignments, subleases, or rights of first offer. Owners should not agree to broad future commissions unless the economics are intentional.

The owner position is that the initial commission covers the initial lease only. A future commission should require a separate written agreement or actual broker participation in negotiating new economics that were not fixed in the original lease.

Owner goal

Prevent a one time leasing assignment from becoming a long term annuity.

Agree to

Agree to no future commissions unless the owner separately agrees in writing.

Compromise

Compromise by paying a reduced commission if the broker actively negotiates a renewal or expansion not already fixed in the lease.

Avoid

Avoid automatic commissions on option renewals, scheduled rent bumps, expansion options, rights of first offer, or amendments negotiated directly by owner and tenant.

No future transaction commission clause

Broker shall not be entitled to any commission or compensation with respect to any renewal, extension, expansion, relocation, contraction, assignment, sublease, license, amendment, modification, surrender, termination, purchase option, right of first offer, right of first refusal, holdover, or other future transaction involving Tenant, the Premises, or the Property unless Owner and Broker enter into a separate written agreement specifically providing for such commission before Broker performs services for that future transaction.

Limited renewal compromise clause

If Broker actively participates at Owner’s written request in negotiating a renewal, extension, or expansion that is not fixed by the original lease, and Owner agrees in writing before those negotiations that Broker will be compensated, Broker shall receive a commission equal to ____ percent of the commission that would otherwise be payable under the initial commission schedule for the applicable renewal, extension, or expansion rent. No commission shall be payable for any renewal, extension, or expansion exercised by Tenant under a right contained in the lease unless Broker materially participates in negotiating economic terms not already determined by the lease.

11. Tail Periods: Keep Them Short and Written

A tail period protects a broker after the agreement expires. A fair tail can make sense where the broker genuinely created an active prospect near the end of the term. But an unlimited or vague tail can block the owner from moving on to a new broker and can create overlapping commission claims.

Owner forms should require a written tail list delivered shortly after expiration, limited to accepted Registered Prospects with substantive activity during the term. The tail should be short, usually ninety to one hundred eighty days depending on transaction type. The broker should not get a tail if terminated for cause.

Owner goal

Protect legitimate broker work without clouding future leasing.

Agree to

Agree to a short tail for accepted Registered Prospects with documented substantive negotiations.

Compromise

Compromise by using a longer tail for large office deals or institutional tenants with long approval cycles.

Avoid

Avoid tails that apply to all tenants contacted, all tenants shown, all prospects in a broker database, or anyone the broker claims to have discussed.

Owner protective tail clause

Within five business days after expiration of the Term, Broker may deliver a written list of accepted Registered Prospects with whom Broker had substantive negotiations during the Term. Broker shall be entitled to a commission during the Tail Period only if Owner accepts the list in writing, Owner enters into a lease with an accepted Registered Prospect within ninety days after expiration, Broker was the procuring cause of the transaction, and the transaction is otherwise commissionable under this Agreement. Broker shall have no post expiration commission rights if Owner terminated this Agreement for cause.

12. Co Brokers and Tenant Representatives

In NYC commercial leasing, the tenant may be represented by its own broker. The owner agreement should state whether the owner will pay the tenant representative directly, whether the listing broker shares its commission, or whether the tenant is responsible for its own representative. If the agreement is silent, the owner may face confusion or duplicate demands.

The owner should not allow the listing broker to promise compensation on the owner’s behalf. The broker should indemnify the owner against all claims by any salesperson, cooperating broker, tenant representative, referral source, or finder claiming through the broker.

Owner goal

Avoid double commission exposure and unauthorized promises.

Agree to

Agree that co broker compensation requires owner’s prior written approval.

Compromise

Compromise by approving a co broker schedule for a specific registered prospect or listing campaign.

Avoid

Avoid allowing the listing broker to advertise owner paid co brokerage terms without written approval.

Co broker clause

Broker shall not offer, promise, advertise, or agree to pay compensation to any co broker, tenant representative, salesperson, associate broker, referral source, consultant, finder, or other person on Owner’s behalf without Owner’s prior written approval. Unless Owner expressly agrees otherwise in writing, Broker shall be solely responsible for compensating any person claiming compensation by, through, or under Broker. Broker shall indemnify, defend, and hold Owner harmless from all claims, demands, commissions, fees, costs, expenses, and liabilities asserted by any person claiming by, through, or under Broker.

13. Marketing Control and No Authority to Bind Owner

Broker marketing can create legal and business problems. A flyer, email blast, online listing, or conversation can imply an asking rent, possession date, use approval, work letter, concession, building condition, or regulatory assurance. Owners should control all public statements about the space.

Owner goal

Control the market message and prevent unauthorized commitments.

Agree to

Agree that broker may market with owner approved materials and must follow approved asking terms.

Compromise

Compromise by preapproving a listing sheet and standard email language.

Avoid

Avoid broker authority to quote final terms, promise concessions, approve uses, accept deposits, or represent building compliance.

Marketing and authority clause

Broker shall not publish, post, distribute, circulate, or transmit any marketing materials, listing information, photographs, floor plans, measurements, asking rents, concessions, availability information, financial information, or other materials concerning the Premises or Property without Owner’s prior approval. Broker has no authority to bind Owner, make representations or warranties on Owner’s behalf, accept deposits, execute documents, approve lease terms, grant concessions, modify asking terms, or make commitments concerning the Premises or Property. All leases, letters of intent, term sheets, concessions, and material communications are subject to Owner’s prior written approval.

14. Use, Licensing, and Building Compliance Statements

NYC commercial leasing often involves regulatory and operational issues. A restaurant tenant may need liquor licensing, venting, grease trap approvals, sidewalk seating, gas, and health department approvals. A medical tenant may need special use analysis, plumbing, waste handling, accessibility, and insurance review. Office and showroom tenants may ask about certificate of occupancy, sprinkler status, elevator work, after hours HVAC, electricity, Local Law compliance, and facade work.

The broker should not make legal, zoning, code, licensing, or building system representations. Those statements should come from the lease, owner, counsel, architect, engineer, expeditor, or other appropriate professional.

Owner goal

Prevent broker statements from becoming owner liability.

Agree to

Agree that broker may share owner approved factual materials only.

Compromise

Compromise by allowing broker to say that a use is subject to owner approval and tenant due diligence.

Avoid

Avoid statements that a use is legal, approvals are routine, capacity is sufficient, or work will be completed by a date unless authorized.

No compliance representations clause

Broker shall not make any representation, warranty, assurance, or statement concerning zoning, permitted use, certificate of occupancy, code compliance, liquor licensing, health department approval, fire safety, sprinkler compliance, accessibility, venting, gas service, electrical capacity, HVAC capacity, Local Law compliance, facade work, building systems, permits, licenses, or governmental approvals unless Owner has expressly approved such statement in writing. Broker may state only that all uses and approvals are subject to Owner approval, lease negotiation, and Tenant’s independent due diligence.

15. Tenant Financial Qualification

A broker’s job should not end with generating interest. Owners need qualified tenants. This is especially important for restaurants, retail, startups, medical uses, nightlife uses, nonprofit tenants, and newly formed entities. The owner agreement should require the broker to gather preliminary credit and operating information, while making clear that the owner has sole approval discretion.

Owner goal

Receive enough information to evaluate credit, use, guaranty, and execution risk.

Agree to

Agree that broker must request financials, ownership information, business history, landlord references, guarantor information, and proposed use details.

Compromise

Compromise by tailoring the required package to the type of tenant and transaction stage.

Avoid

Avoid spending legal and design resources on tenants with no financial capacity or unsuitable use.

Tenant qualification clause

Broker shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain information reasonably requested by Owner concerning each prospective tenant, including ownership information, business history, financial statements, tax returns, bank references, landlord references, guarantor information, proposed use, licensing requirements, operating history, source of funds, and intended occupancy schedule. Owner shall have sole and absolute discretion to accept, reject, or counter any prospect based on credit, use, reputation, financial capacity, guaranty support, building compatibility, proposed terms, or any other lawful reason.

16. Letters of Intent and Term Sheets

Brokers commonly help negotiate letters of intent. Owners should make clear that an LOI is not a lease, not a binding commitment unless expressly stated, and not a commission trigger. The broker should not use an LOI to claim that the deal was complete.

Owner goal

Keep business discussions from becoming binding commitments or commission triggers.

Agree to

Agree that broker may assist with nonbinding term sheets at owner’s direction.

Compromise

Compromise by allowing a commission trigger at lease execution, not rent commencement, only for strong credit tenants with security delivered.

Avoid

Avoid commission rights based on an LOI, email, handshake, draft lease, or agreement in principle.

LOI clause

Broker may assist in preparing or negotiating nonbinding letters of intent, proposals, or term sheets only at Owner’s direction. No letter of intent, proposal, term sheet, counterproposal, email, oral agreement, draft lease, or agreement in principle shall obligate Owner to enter into a lease or pay any commission. Commission rights shall arise only if a lease is fully executed and all conditions to commission payment under this Agreement are satisfied.

17. Term and Termination

Owners should be wary of long exclusive terms without performance obligations. A broker may want enough time to market the property, but the owner needs an exit if the broker is inactive, ignores approved strategy, mishandles prospects, makes unauthorized statements, or creates conflicts.

Owner goal

Keep control if the broker is not performing or creates risk.

Agree to

Agree to a defined term with termination for convenience and immediate termination for cause.

Compromise

Compromise by using a short initial exclusive period followed by extension only if activity milestones are met.

Avoid

Avoid automatic renewals or termination rights that preserve broad commission claims without a clear tail list.

Termination clause

This Agreement shall commence on __________ and expire on __________ unless earlier terminated in accordance with this Agreement. Owner may terminate this Agreement for convenience upon fifteen days’ written notice. Owner may terminate immediately upon written notice if Broker breaches this Agreement, makes unauthorized representations, violates applicable law, loses or suspends a required license, fails to maintain required insurance, breaches confidentiality, creates a conflict of interest, engages in misconduct, or acts in a manner that Owner reasonably determines may harm Owner, the Premises, or the Property. Upon expiration or termination, Broker shall immediately cease marketing the Premises, remove all listings and marketing materials, return or destroy confidential information, and deliver pending prospect information requested by Owner.

18. Broker Licensing, Agency Disclosure, and Compliance

New York Real Property Law Article 12 A governs real estate brokers and salespersons, including licensing requirements and actions for commissions. New York Department of State resources also include real estate broker forms and agency disclosure forms for landlord and tenant relationships. Owners should require the broker to represent that all brokerage services will be performed by properly licensed persons and that all agency disclosures and legal requirements will be satisfied.

Owner goal

Make legal compliance the broker’s responsibility.

Agree to

Agree that broker must be licensed, supervise salespersons, provide required disclosures, and comply with advertising and agency rules.

Compromise

Compromise by attaching the required disclosure form when applicable and requiring broker to maintain records.

Avoid

Avoid using unlicensed finders, informal referral sources, or consultants who expect commission like brokers.

Licensing and compliance clause

Broker represents and warrants that Broker and all persons performing brokerage services under this Agreement are duly licensed to the extent required by applicable law. Broker shall comply with all laws, rules, regulations, licensing requirements, agency disclosure requirements, advertising requirements, antidiscrimination laws, professional standards, and Department of State requirements applicable to Broker’s services. Broker shall not share compensation with any person in violation of applicable law.

19. Indemnity and Insurance

The indemnity should protect the owner from broker caused problems: unauthorized representations, advertising errors, licensing violations, confidentiality breaches, discrimination claims caused by broker conduct, co broker claims, salesperson claims, referral claims, and commission claims from anyone claiming through the broker.

Owner goal

Shift broker controlled risks back to the broker.

Agree to

Agree to broker indemnity for misconduct, breach, unauthorized statements, legal violations, and claims by brokers claiming through broker.

Compromise

Compromise by making indemnity mutual only for each party’s own breach or misconduct.

Avoid

Avoid a one sided owner indemnity for all commission disputes, especially where claims arise from broker’s network.

Indemnity clause

Broker shall indemnify, defend, and hold Owner and its affiliates, members, managers, partners, officers, directors, agents, lenders, property managers, and representatives harmless from and against all claims, damages, losses, liabilities, commissions, fines, penalties, costs, and expenses, including reasonable attorneys’ fees, arising out of or relating to Broker’s breach of this Agreement, unauthorized representation, negligence, willful misconduct, violation of law, advertising, disclosure of confidential information, failure to provide required disclosures, or any claim for compensation by a person claiming by, through, or under Broker.

Insurance clause

Broker shall maintain commercially reasonable insurance coverage, including commercial general liability insurance and errors and omissions insurance, with limits reasonably acceptable to Owner. Broker shall provide certificates of insurance upon Owner’s request. Broker’s insurance obligations shall not limit Broker’s indemnity obligations.

20. Confidentiality and Information Control

Commercial leasing often requires the owner to disclose sensitive information: plans, measurements, rent rolls, tenant mix, financing issues, planned capital work, litigation, violations, concessions, and asking strategy. Brokers should use that information only for approved leasing efforts.

Owner goal

Protect nonpublic business and building information.

Agree to

Agree that broker may disclose approved materials to bona fide prospects and tenant reps who need the information.

Compromise

Compromise by allowing disclosure after an NDA or written broker confirmation of confidentiality.

Avoid

Avoid free circulation of plans, financials, rent rolls, tenant issues, or owner strategy.

Confidentiality clause

Broker shall keep confidential all nonpublic information concerning Owner, the Property, the Premises, tenants, lease terms, rent rolls, financial information, building plans, ownership structure, financing, negotiations, violations, capital projects, concessions, and prospective transactions. Broker shall use confidential information solely to perform services under this Agreement and shall not disclose such information except to bona fide prospective tenants and tenant representatives who have a need to know and only to the extent disclosure has been approved by Owner or is reasonably necessary for approved marketing.

21. Competing Broker Claims and Escrow Rights

Multiple broker claims are common in active NYC leasing. One broker may register the tenant, another may show the space, a tenant representative may appear later, or a salesperson may move firms. The owner should not be forced to decide disputed broker claims at its own risk.

Owner goal

Prevent duplicate payment and preserve leverage until claims are resolved.

Agree to

Agree that owner may withhold disputed commissions until competing claims are resolved.

Compromise

Compromise by allowing payment of the undisputed portion and escrow of the disputed portion.

Avoid

Avoid any provision requiring owner to pay first and recover later from brokers.

Competing claims clause

If any person or entity asserts a claim for commission or compensation in connection with a transaction covered by this Agreement, Owner may withhold payment of any disputed commission until the dispute is resolved by written agreement, final judgment, arbitration award, or other binding resolution. Owner may deposit the disputed amount into escrow or court if Owner determines that doing so is appropriate. Broker shall cooperate with Owner in resolving any competing claim and shall indemnify Owner against claims by any person claiming by, through, or under Broker.

22. Deal Type Specific Owner Concerns

Retail and restaurant leasing

The agreement should address use sensitivity. The broker should not promise liquor licensing, venting, grease trap feasibility, sidewalk seating, hours of operation, delivery rights, trash storage, signage approval, gas availability, or outdoor space. The owner should require a tenant financial and operating package before legal negotiation.

Office leasing

The broker should not quote loss factor, usable area, work letter scope, tenant improvement allowance, possession date, after hours HVAC rates, electric capacity, or furniture inclusion unless owner approves those statements. Owners should be careful with commissions on expansion options and renewal rights.

Medical and specialty use

The broker should be required to identify licensing needs, waste issues, plumbing demands, patient traffic, accessibility needs, elevator patterns, and hours. The broker should not assure the tenant that the building can accommodate the use.

Warehouse, showroom, and light industrial

The broker should not represent loading capacity, freight elevator capacity, floor load, sprinkler classification, certificate of occupancy, truck access, hazardous materials compatibility, or utility capacity without owner approved materials.

24. Sample Owner Protective Nonexclusive Broker Agreement

The following short form is intended as a starting point for an owner that wants to let a broker submit prospects without giving exclusivity. It should be adapted to the transaction, property, broker relationship, and applicable law.

Short form agreement

1. Parties. This Owner Broker Agreement is entered into as of __________ by and between __________, as owner or authorized agent for owner, and __________, licensed real estate broker.

2. Premises. This Agreement applies only to the premises described on Exhibit A at __________, New York, New York.

3. Appointment. Owner authorizes Broker on a nonexclusive basis to submit prospective tenants for the Premises. Broker is not Owner’s exclusive leasing agent and does not have an exclusive right to lease. Owner may lease the Premises directly or through other brokers without liability to Broker except as expressly provided in this Agreement.

4. Registration. Broker shall register each prospective tenant in writing before showing the Premises or commencing substantive discussions. A prospect becomes a Registered Prospect only if Owner accepts the registration in writing. Owner may reject any registration for any lawful reason, including that the prospect is known to Owner, previously registered, previously submitted, already in discussions, financially unsuitable, or an Excluded Prospect.

5. Excluded Prospects. Broker shall not be entitled to compensation with respect to existing tenants, subtenants, occupants, licensees, affiliates of the foregoing, prospects listed on Exhibit B, prospects previously submitted by another broker, prospects already in discussions with Owner, or prospects that contact Owner directly without Broker’s involvement.

6. Commission. If Owner enters into a lease with an accepted Registered Prospect during the term of this Agreement and Broker is the procuring cause of the transaction, Owner shall pay Broker a commission equal to __________. The commission shall be calculated only on fixed base rent actually payable during the initial lease term and shall exclude free rent, abatements, concessions, additional rent, taxes, operating expenses, utilities, percentage rent, tenant improvement reimbursements, signage fees, storage fees, parking fees, termination payments, and all other amounts.

7. Payment Conditions. No commission is earned or payable unless a lease is fully executed and delivered, Tenant delivers all required security deposits, guaranties, letters of credit, insurance certificates, and initial payments, all lease effectiveness conditions are satisfied, and Tenant takes possession. The commission shall be payable fifty percent on rent commencement and fifty percent six months after rent commencement, provided Tenant is not then in default beyond applicable notice and cure periods.

8. No Future Commissions. Broker is not entitled to commissions on renewals, extensions, expansions, relocations, assignments, subleases, licenses, amendments, surrender agreements, termination payments, rights of first offer, rights of first refusal, purchase rights, or other future transactions unless Owner agrees in a separate writing.

9. Co Brokers. Broker is solely responsible for compensating any co broker, tenant representative, salesperson, referral source, or other person claiming through Broker unless Owner expressly agrees otherwise in writing.

10. No Authority. Broker has no authority to bind Owner, make representations, accept deposits, approve terms, execute documents, or make commitments on Owner’s behalf. All terms are subject to Owner’s approval.

11. Marketing. Broker shall not publish, post, distribute, or transmit marketing materials, rent quotes, floor plans, photographs, financial information, or availability information without Owner’s approval.

12. Confidentiality. Broker shall keep all nonpublic information confidential and shall use such information solely for purposes of this Agreement.

13. Termination. This Agreement begins on __________ and expires on __________. Owner may terminate this Agreement at any time upon fifteen days’ written notice and immediately for cause.

14. Tail. Broker has no post expiration commission rights except for accepted Registered Prospects listed by Broker within five business days after expiration and accepted by Owner in writing. Any tail right expires ninety days after expiration and applies only if Broker was the procuring cause.

15. Indemnity. Broker shall indemnify Owner from claims arising from Broker’s breach, unauthorized representations, violation of law, misconduct, negligence, confidentiality breach, or any claim for compensation by a person claiming by, through, or under Broker.

16. Governing Law and Forum. This Agreement is governed by New York law. Any action arising out of this Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in New York County, New York.

17. Entire Agreement. This Agreement is the entire agreement between Owner and Broker and may be amended only by a writing signed by Owner and Broker.

25. Owner Checklist Before Signing

Checklist

Does the agreement say exclusive right, exclusive agency, or nonexclusive authorization?

Does the covered premises description exclude other building areas and future vacancies?

Is there a written Excluded Prospect exhibit?

Does the broker need written acceptance of tenant registrations?

Does registration expire after a defined period?

Is commission calculated only on fixed base rent actually payable?

Are free rent, abatements, additional rent, taxes, expenses, utilities, reimbursements, security, and letters of credit excluded?

Is payment conditioned on signed lease, security delivery, possession, and rent commencement?

Is there installment payment or clawback protection for early default?

Are renewals, expansions, relocations, amendments, and future transactions excluded?

Is the tail period limited to a short written list of accepted registered prospects?

Is there no tail after termination for cause?

Does owner approve all co brokers and tenant rep payments?

Does broker indemnify owner for claims by brokers claiming through broker?

Does broker lack authority to bind owner or make use and compliance statements?

Does owner control all marketing materials and listings?

Does owner have termination for convenience and immediate termination for cause?

Does the agreement include New York governing law and New York County forum if desired?

26. Practical Takeaway

An owner broker agreement should do more than state a commission rate. It should function as a risk allocation document. It should identify exactly what the broker is hired to do, what transactions are commissionable, what transactions are excluded, when commission rights arise, when payment is made, and what happens if the lease fails or the broker relationship ends.

The owner’s best position is not to avoid paying brokers. The best position is to pay a broker who produces a real tenant on agreed terms, while preventing avoidable disputes over owner sourced tenants, existing prospects, future renewals, additional rent, co broker claims, and post expiration tails.

For most owner side NYC commercial leasing assignments, the safest starting point is either a nonexclusive authorization or a carefully limited exclusive agency. A broad exclusive right to lease should be used only when the owner intentionally accepts the possibility of paying a commission on any lease signed during the term, subject to carefully drafted exclusions.
