Construction & Design Contracts

A construction project is an exercise in allocating risk among parties whose interests only partly align, and every tension is resolved, well or poorly, in the contracts signed before the first shovel enters the ground. We represent owners, architects and engineers, and contractors and subcontractors, in separate engagements, and that breadth makes us better counsel to whichever side we serve.

Owners. We advise on the choice of delivery method (design-bid-build, design-build, or construction manager) and negotiate the owner-architect and owner-contractor agreements, focusing on scope and standard of work, price and payment, schedule and delay remedies, the change-order process, insurance, indemnity, and warranties, and rights to terminate and to the design documents.

Architects & design professionals. We protect the distinction of a firm that sells judgment rather than a finished structure. That means holding the standard of care to reasonable care rather than a guarantee of perfection, and addressing scope of services, ownership of the instruments of service, certifications, and limitation-of-liability terms that keep exposure proportionate to the fee.

Contractors & subcontractors. We negotiate prime contracts, subcontracts, and the flow-down provisions that quietly allocate risk: payment and retainage, change orders and differing-site conditions, delay and acceleration (including no-damage-for-delay clauses), and the lien and bond rights that are a builder's ultimate security for payment.

New York's particular rules. We draft with New York's statutory overlay in view: the Lien Law trust-fund rules that govern how project money moves, the limits on indemnification that void clauses purporting to indemnify a party against its own negligence, the prompt-payment rules, and the strict mechanic's-lien deadlines that are unforgiving of a missed date. When projects go wrong, we represent clients in payment and lien disputes, delay and disruption claims, defect and standard-of-care claims, and termination conflicts.
